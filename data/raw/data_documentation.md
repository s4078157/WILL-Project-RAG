##### \[P1] - Van Dongen et al.

Title: The Cumulative Cost of Additional Wakefulness: Dose-Response Effects on 

Neurobehavioral Functions and Sleep Physiology From Chronic Sleep Restriction

and Total Sleep Deprivation



Journal: Sleep



Topic\_tag: `sleep\_restriction`



Reason Selected:

This paper studies how different amounts of lost sleep and no sleep affect thinking and attention. It is useful for the RAG because it shows that not getting enough sleep repeatedly can hurt how well you focus and think.



Example Question:

* What happens to cognitive performance when someone regularly gets too little sleep?
* If a person sleeps less than usual for a few nights in a row, does it impact their attention?
* How is getting some sleep, but not enough, different from not sleeping at all?
* Is it possible for lost sleep to add up over time and make people less alert?

##### \[P4] - Ward et al.

Title: Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity



Journal: Journal of the Association for Consumer Research  



Topic\_tag: `smartphone\_presence\_cognition`



Reason Selected:

This paper looks at whether just having your smartphone nearby changes how well you can think, even if you are not using it. It is useful for the RAG because it shows, through experiments, how everyday technology might impact memory and thinking skills



Example Question:

* Does the presence of a smartphone have a reducing effect on a person's cognitive performance even if it is not being used?
* How does the location of a smartphone affect working memory and cognitive capacity?
* Does it do more to improve cognitive performance to keep the smartphone in another room than to keep it on the desk or in a pocket?
* Why does the simple fact of having a smartphone around reduce cognitive ability even if a person is not deliberately thinking about the phone?
